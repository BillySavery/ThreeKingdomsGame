    How to play. Five battles a day from a pool of over 100, with no repeats until you've seen them all. Tap where you think each one was fought and lock it in. Pinch, scroll or use the buttons to zoom in, and drag to move around. You score up to 1,000 points a battle, less the further away you land: within 50 km scores over 800, and 500 km away scores under 150. The rivers, capitals and the names of the Han provinces are there to help you find your bearings, and dotted lines show today's provinces for anyone who knows the modern map better. Switch on Han borders to swap them for the Han provinces around 140 AD instead. These are approximate: each area goes to its nearest commandery seat, using the seats in Wikipedia's list of Han commanderies, so real borders that followed rivers and mountains won't match exactly. Borders have moved and rivers have changed course since then, but the mountains haven't, so you can switch on the terrain to read the land the way the generals did. It costs nothing, but a ⛰️ in your shared result shows you used it. The Yellow River is drawn on its course of the time, which reached the sea north of the Shandong hills. After each pin, the map shades in Wei, Shu and Wu as they stood around 230; before 220 those kingdoms didn't exist yet, so treat it as a rough guide to who later held the ground. Locations follow the most widely accepted modern sites; a few, like Red Cliffs and Jieting, are still argued over.

    Fan made. Not affiliated with Koei Tecmo. Coastline and terrain from Natural Earth. Han borders approximate, built from the commandery seats in Wikipedia's List of provinces and commanderies of the Han dynasty.
